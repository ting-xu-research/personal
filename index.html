About

Now

I am a Scientist at A*STAR in Singapore, working with Dr. Jean Wei-Jun Yeung on the Singapore Longitudinal Early Development Study (SG-LEADS). Using the cohort's time diaries and repeated waves, I examine young children's screen time, whether families meet WHO and AAP screen-time guidelines, and how early screen exposure relates to later cognitive, memory, and socio-emotional outcomes.

PhD

I earned my PhD in Family Social Science from the University of Minnesota in 2026, advised by Drs. Xiaoran Sun and Zha Blong Xiong. Much of my doctoral work used Screenomics data: screenshots captured every few seconds while a phone is on, turned into measures of when, how long, and on what adolescents and parents use their phones. My dissertation examined objective smartphone use and perceived smartphone addiction among adolescents and their parents, using longitudinal multilevel models.

Research

Current focus · SG-LEADS

Early childhood screen time and development in Singapore

Time-diary measures of young children's screen use, how many families meet WHO and AAP screen-time guidelines at different ages, and links to later cognitive, memory, and behavioral outcomes.

Screenomics

What adolescents and parents really do on their phones

Smartphone use during school hours and overnight, measured with high-intensity screenshot data, compared with self-reported smartphone addiction.

Family systems

Parallel screens in parent–adolescent dyads

How parents' and teens' phone use move together, and how parent–adolescent relationships relate to smartphone addiction.

Health & wellbeing

Media, body image, and health behavior

Social media use and weight concerns, and media messages and adolescents' preventive health behaviors.

Culture & context

Immigrant families and community schools

Social capital and academic competence among students in Hmongcentric charter schools, and parental control across racial groups.
